BEYOND THE SURFACE

Inside the Deep Sea

Learn how extraordinary deep-sea animals survive in one of Earth's most challenging environments.

01 / BIOLUMINESCENCE

Anglerfish

Some deep-sea anglerfish have a special structure that extends from the head and acts as a lure to attract prey.

Habitat

Different species live in different ocean environments, including the deep sea.

Adaptation

In certain species, the lure produces light with the help of bioluminescent bacteria.

Fun Fact

In some deep-sea anglerfish, the much smaller male attaches to the female as part of reproduction.

02 / DEEP-SEA SURVIVOR

Vampire Squid

Despite its name, the vampire squid is not a blood-drinking vampire. It is a deep-sea cephalopod adapted to low-oxygen waters.

Habitat

It lives in deep ocean waters where oxygen levels can be very low.

Adaptation

Its low-energy lifestyle helps it survive in an environment with limited food.

Fun Fact

It can turn its webbed arms outward to cover much of its body when threatened.

03 / UNUSUAL ADAPTATION

Dumbo Octopus

Dumbo octopuses are deep-sea animals named after the ear-like fins on their heads.

Habitat

They live in deep ocean environments, including areas near the seafloor.

Adaptation

Their fins help them swim, while their flexible arms help them move and handle food.

Fun Fact

Unlike many other octopuses, Dumbo octopuses mainly use their fins to move through the water.

04 / LIGHT PRODUCER

Lanternfish

Lanternfish are small marine fish known for the light-producing organs found on their bodies.

Habitat

They are found in the open ocean, including many species that live in deep waters.

Adaptation

Their light-producing organs can help with camouflage and communication.

Fun Fact

Lanternfish are an important part of ocean food webs and are eaten by many larger animals.

05 / Abyssal Scavenger

Giant Isopod

Habitat

Giant isopods live on the deep-sea floor, usually at depths where sunlight cannot reach.

Adaptation

Their hard exoskeleton protects their bodies. They can also survive for long periods without food.

Fun Fact

Giant isopods are related to familiar land-dwelling woodlice, but they grow much larger.

06 / Pelican Mouth

Gulper Eel

Habitat

Gulper eels inhabit deep ocean waters, far below the sunlit surface.

Adaptation

Their enormous mouths and expandable stomachs help them swallow prey that may be surprisingly large.

Fun Fact

The gulper eel's mouth can look much larger than the rest of its slender body.

07 / Transparent Head

Barreleye Fish

Habitat

Barreleye fish live in deep ocean waters, often where very little natural light is available.

Adaptation

Their transparent head helps protect their eyes, which can rotate to look upward or forward.

Fun Fact

The barreleye fish has unusual tube-shaped eyes that help it detect prey in dim light.

08 / Seabed Stilt-Walker

Tripod Fish

Habitat

Tripod fish live on the deep-sea floor, where sunlight does not reach.

Adaptation

They use long, stiff fins to support themselves above the seabed while waiting for food.

Fun Fact

Their long fins resemble a tripod, which gives this unusual fish its name.
